## Analysis
When only the app container died (not the whole pod):
- Consul's HTTP health check started failing (5s interval, ~2 failures needed)
- After ~10-15s, Consul marked the service as critical
- Envoy removed the endpoint from its load balancer
- All subsequent requests routed to backend-1 and backend-3
- 267 requests returned 502 during the detection window

## Conclusion
Health-check-based failover works but introduces a ~10-15s detection
window during which a small percentage of requests fail (1.5% in this
test). Compared to Phase 2 (pod kill = 0 errors, instant detection),
this scenario is more representative of real-world application crashes
where the process dies but the container/network stays alive.

Trade-off: health checks are more thorough (catch hung/broken apps)
but slower to detect than TCP-level failures.
